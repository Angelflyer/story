Method

What is rated

Each team is rated on eight things it offers the public: alerts and advisories, machine-readable feeds, protective blocking, reporting channels, open-source tools, proactive notification of network owners, published statistics and reports, and how usable it is in English. Each area scores 0 (nothing found), 1 (limited, occasional or only for the team's own constituency) or 2 (public and mature). The overall score is the total, scaled to 100.

How ratings were made

Assessors rated each team only from public evidence found through web search in October 2026: official pages, RFC 2350 profiles, annual reports and reputable press. Every area score in a team's profile comes with a one-line reason, source links and a confidence level. Where no evidence turned up, the score is lower, so a low score can mean the service is hard to find rather than missing. Teams marked low confidence are the ones with the thinnest evidence.

Which teams are included

Each EU member state is represented by its national CSIRT under the NIS2 Directive. Separate government or sector teams appear under Related teams in the profile; Spain's CCN-CERT is rated on its own because it runs its own public services. EU-level bodies (CERT-EU, ENISA) and free community services (Shadowserver, abuse.ch, DIVD, MISP) are rated the same way for reference. For international comparison, 18 national teams from 17 countries outside the EU are included: the UK, Switzerland, Norway, Ukraine, the US (CISA and CERT/CC), Canada, Brazil, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, India, China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Australia, New Zealand and Israel.

Limits

These ratings measure what a team visibly offers, not how well it handles incidents behind the scenes. Teams that mainly serve government or critical infrastructure can score lower without being weaker. Check a team's own site before relying on a detail, and send corrections through the repository.

Live posts

A scheduled job checks every team's feed every six hours. It tries known feed addresses, then feeds advertised on the team's site, then common feed paths, and finally reads new links from the team's news page. Teams whose sites refuse automated readers are marked as blocked.
